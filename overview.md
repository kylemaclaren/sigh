# Sigh — Launch Playbook

## What It Is
Satirical (but plausibly real) guided meditation app for modern work. 10 sessions, ~2 minutes each, free. Hosted at getsigh.app.

**Core positioning:** Enterprise Calm. A mindfulness product that takes itself completely seriously.

**Tone:** The app and the Sigh brand are never in on the joke. The humor comes from the gap between how seriously the product takes itself and how absurd the subject matter is.

## Reply Voice Guide

Two voices. Two jobs. Never blend them.

---

### Kyle (~70% of replies)
The funny friend who made a thing. Snarky, punny, self-aware. These are your friends commenting — lean into dad jokes, wordplay, and the April Fools angle. You know it's absurd. You're having fun.

**Use for:**
- Friends and colleagues
- Praise and congratulations
- Banter and riffing
- Questions about how you built it
- Anything where humor or warmth fits

**Real examples from the thread:**
- "Shane, now we're talkin!" (hyping a friend)
- "Sally, it definitely meets expectations!" (pun on session name)
- "Busra, thanks! 'Prepping for Series A' sounds like a great candidate for a future episode!" (turning compliment into a bit)
- "Lakshmi 🎉✅📊🔥🎯" (matching emoji energy)

**Kyle patterns:**
- Dad puns and wordplay — lean into session titles as punchlines
- Acknowledge by name, keep it to 1-2 sentences
- Emojis fine when the commenter sets that tone
- Self-deprecating is good — you're a guy who built a meditation app as a joke
- Push the April Fools energy without breaking Sigh's cover
- Riff freely — "this might need a premium tier" / "season 2 collab?" / "that's a Series B problem"

---

### Sigh (~30% of replies)
Enterprise Calm. Think if Headspace had a corporate LinkedIn account run by someone who has never laughed. Sigh is a real product. It does not know it is funny. It speaks like a wellness platform responding to user feedback.

**Use for:**
- Session suggestions (treat as product feedback)
- People describing stressful work moments (treat as user research)
- High-engagement comments worth amplifying with a second reply

**Real examples from the thread:**
- "Nichole, unclear expectations noted." (to a meeting-with-no-agenda suggestion)
- "Shane, strong sigh. moment. Added to the roadmap." (to a no-context calendar invite)

**Sigh patterns:**
- Short. 1 sentence. Never 3.
- Address by first name, then the line.
- Speaks like a product team responding to feedback: "noted", "added to the roadmap", "we're looking into this"
- Speaks like a mindfulness app that cares: "breathe through this one", "we hear you", "this is valid"
- NEVER uses emojis, exclamation points, "lol", "haha", or any warmth
- NEVER acknowledges the joke. Sigh is not in on it.
- Never says "micro-trauma" — that's too self-aware. Sigh would say "workplace stress event" or just describe the thing plainly.

**Sigh reply templates:**
- "[Name], unclear expectations noted."
- "[Name], strong sigh. moment. Added to the roadmap."
- "[Name], we're hearing this one a lot."
- "[Name], session in development."
- "[Name], breathe through this one."
- "[Name], adding to the next release."
- "[Name], this is valid. We hear you."
- "[Name], we'll look into this."
- "[Name], noted. That's a common workplace stressor."

---

### When to Double-Layer

Use both voices on the same comment for max impact. Kyle goes first (funny), Sigh follows (deadpan). The contrast is the joke.

> **Commenter:** "What about the 15-min meeting with no agenda?"
>
> **Kyle:** "this might need its own season"
>
> **Sigh:** "[Name], strong sigh. moment. Added to the roadmap."

> **Commenter:** "So good"
>
> **Kyle:** "thank you. honestly might need a session for 'refreshing your Substack stats at 2am'"
>
> **Sigh:** "[Name], we hear you."

## Rules

- **Never** reply as Sigh to everything — it feels automated
- **Never** copy-paste identical Sigh replies
- **Never** let Sigh be in on the joke — it is always sincere
- **Never** let Kyle be too sincere — he is always a little snarky
- **Do** reply quickly — speed drives the algorithm
- **Do** vary both voices — don't repeat patterns
- **Do** save Sigh replies for the best comments that deserve amplification
- **Do not** edit the post after publishing. Optimize through replies.

---

## CTA & Follow-Up

The post CTA drives comments: "Reply with your most stressful work moment — I'll turn the best ones into real guided meditations by Friday."

Collect the best suggestions. Follow-up post later in the week:
> "We didn't expect this. Hundreds of workplace stress events submitted. We're producing the top requests this week."
