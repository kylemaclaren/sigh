## Session 1 — You Are Not an LLM

Welcome to Sigh. 

Today's session is for a specific kind of stress. The kind that arrives in the form of a LinkedIn post, a company memo, or a casual comment from your manager that begins with "Have you seen what AI can do now?"

Take a deep breath. [exhale] You are here. You are real. You are not a large language model. [long pause]

Let that sink in for a moment. [long pause]

Now. I want you to think about the thing you do at work. The thing you're good at. Maybe it's writing. Maybe it's organizing. Maybe it's knowing which stakeholder to loop in and which one to quietly leave off the email. [long pause]

An AI cannot do that. Not really. It can approximate it. It can generate something that looks like it. But it doesn't know why Karen from Legal needs to be cc'd but never replied to directly. It doesn't know that.

You know that. [long pause]

Take another breath. [exhale]

The fear you're feeling is valid. It's not irrational. The world is changing, and it's changing fast. But you've adapted before. You learned email. You learned Slack. You learned to unmute yourself on Zoom. Eventually. [long pause]

You will adapt again.

And in the meantime, if an AI writes something and someone asks, "Did you write this?" -- you are allowed to pause. You are allowed to feel complicated about it. That complexity is human. Lean into it. [long pause]

Take one more breath. [exhale]

You are not a prompt. You are not a workflow. You are a person who sometimes forgets to save, and that's okay. [long pause]

This has been Sigh. You're doing better than the algorithm thinks.

## Session 2 — Quick Question

Welcome to Sigh.

Today's session is for a very specific moment. The moment you see those two words appear on your screen: "Quick question."

Take a breath. [exhale] You already know it's not going to be quick. That's okay. Let's sit with that knowledge together.

"Quick question" is never one question. It is a door. And behind that door is another door. And behind that door is a meeting invite. This is the natural order of things. [pause]

Notice where you feel it in your body. Maybe it's your jaw tightening. Maybe it's your shoulders rising toward your ears. Maybe it's the involuntary glance at your calendar to see if you have an excuse. [long pause]

All of these responses are valid. You are a person with boundaries. Boundaries that are about to be gently ignored.

Take another breath. [exhale]

The message will unfold in stages. First comes the question. It will not be quick. Then comes the follow-up: "Actually, one more thing." Then: "Sorry, last one." It will not be the last one. It is never the last one. Accept this. [long pause]

When the message arrives, do not respond immediately. Let it sit. Not out of spite. Out of self-preservation. You are allowed to finish your thought before starting someone else's. [pause]

You are not a search engine. You are not on-call. You are a person who was doing something before this message arrived, and you will return to that something. Eventually.

This has been Sigh. It was just a quick session.

## Session 3 — 2,847 Unread

Welcome to Sigh.

I'd like you to look at your email. Not open it. Just... look at the icon. [pause] Notice the number. The red badge. The unread count.

What does that number make you feel? Guilt? Anxiety? A vague sense that you're failing at something you never signed up for?

That number is not a judgment. It is just a number. A very large number. But still just a number.

Here's something they don't teach you in onboarding: you will never reach zero. Inbox Zero is not a destination. It is a concept, like world peace or a clean garage. Beautiful in theory. Unattainable in practice.

And that's okay. [pause]

Every email in your inbox is a tiny request from another person who is also overwhelmed. You are connected by overwhelm. There's something almost beautiful about that. Almost.

So here's what we're going to do. Take a breath. [exhale] Now, select the oldest email in your inbox. The one from 2024. The one you've been meaning to reply to. [pause]

Archive it. Don't reply. Just archive it. If it was truly important, they would have followed up. And if they did follow up, that follow-up is also buried in your inbox, and that's a problem for another session.

This has been Sigh. You have 2,847 unread messages. And you are enough.

## Session 4 — Meets Expectations

Welcome to Sigh.

It's that time. You know what time. The document is sitting in your inbox, or maybe it's a link in a Slack message with a smiley face emoji that tells you nothing.

Before you open it... let's take a moment.

Breathe in. [inhale] Breathe out. [exhale]

You are more than a rating. Say that quietly to yourself. [pause] You don't have to believe it yet. Just say it.

Now. Let's prepare. When you open the document, you're going to see words. Some of those words will feel good. Let them in. Don't skip past the good parts to find the bad parts. [pause] I know that's what you want to do. Resist.

Some of the words might sting. That's okay. A performance review is one person's perspective, shaped by a rubric they also don't fully understand, delivered on a timeline set by HR.

It is not the truth of who you are. It is a document. And you already know how to deal with documents.

When you're ready, open it. Read it slowly. If you need to stop, stop. If you need to close the laptop and take a walk, do that. If you need to text a friend "can you believe this," that's valid too.

And when it's over... remember: you've survived every review you've ever received. Your record is perfect.

This has been Sigh. We believe in you. Even in Q2.

## Session 5 — Your 7th Manager

Welcome to Sigh.

Today we're going to sit with a truth that many of us find difficult to accept: your org chart is not permanent.

Take a breath. [exhale]

In Buddhism, they teach that nothing is permanent. Not the seasons. Not the self. And certainly not your reporting structure.

You may have a manager today who understands your work. [pause] Cherish them. They may be reorged into a different pillar by Thursday. This is not personal. It is the natural order of corporate impermanence.

Perhaps you've been through a reorg before. Perhaps you've been through several. Perhaps you've been "aligned to a new function" so many times that you've lost track of which Slack channel is your team's real channel.

That's okay. You are not your team name. You are not your cost center. You are a person who happens to sit inside a box on a slide that someone in Strategy is probably moving right now.

Let go of the box. You were never the box.

Take one more breath. [exhale]

Whatever happens, you will update your Slack status, you will join the new standup, and you will carry on. You always do.

This has been Sigh. May your next manager also approve your PTO.

## Session 6 — Your Camera Is Off

Welcome to Sigh.

You're about to join an All-Hands meeting. Or perhaps you're already in one. If so, no one can see your screen. You're safe here.

Take a breath. [exhale] Find a comfortable position. You're going to be here for a while.

The All-Hands is a unique challenge for the mindful professional. It asks you to be present -- truly present -- for 45 to 90 minutes of content that may or may not apply to your role, your team, or your continent. [pause]

This is hard. We acknowledge that. [pause]

When your attention drifts -- and it will drift -- do not fight it. Simply notice it. "Ah, I'm thinking about lunch." That's okay. Gently bring your focus back to the slide. [pause] It's a bar chart. You've seen it before. That's okay too.

If someone says "I'll keep this brief," prepare yourself. Take a breath. This is a test of your practice. It will not be brief. It never is. Accept this. Find peace in it.

If there is a Q&A section, you do not have to ask a question. Silence is a valid form of participation. You are contributing by being here. Mostly.

And when it's over -- when the "Thanks, everyone!" arrives -- allow yourself a moment of gratitude. You made it. [pause] You survived. The recording will be sent out later, and you will never watch it, and that is perfectly fine.

This has been Sigh. Your camera was off and no one noticed.

### Share Card

## Session 8 — Share Your Screen

Welcome to Sigh. 

Today's session is for a moment that strikes without warning. Someone on the call has just said five words: "Can you share your screen?"

Take a breath. [exhale] We have about three seconds before you need to answer. Let's use them wisely.

First: the scan. What is on your screen right now? [pause] How many tabs do you have open? If the answer is "I don't know," that's too many. If one of those tabs is a job listing, a recipe, or something you Googled at 2 AM... we'll deal with that in a moment. Slack DMs visible in the corner? Close them. Close them now. [pause]

Good. Let's breathe. [exhale]

You have a choice. Two paths. Full screen, or share just this window. [long pause]

Full screen is brave. It says: "I have nothing to hide." It is a lie, but a confident one. Window sharing is safer. It says: "I am organized enough to curate your experience." Also a lie. Both are valid. [pause]

Whatever you choose, know this: the notification will come. It always comes. A Slack message. A calendar reminder. A text from your mother. You cannot prevent it. You can only breathe through it.

And here is a truth that may comfort you: everyone's screen looks like this. Every single person on this call has 40 tabs open, an overdue Jira ticket, and a screenshot from 2023 they can't explain. You are not alone in your chaos. You are connected by it. [long pause]

Now share your screen. You're ready. Or share your window. That's ready too.

This has been Sigh. Full screen is brave. Window sharing is cowardice. Both are valid choices.

## Session 10 — No Times Work

Welcome to Sigh.

Today we're going to work through something that tests even the most practiced among us: scheduling a meeting.

Take a breath. [exhale] You're going to need your patience for this one.

It begins simply enough. Someone says, "Let's find time to connect." A reasonable request. And yet what follows will consume more energy than the meeting itself. [pause]

You open your calendar. You find three slots. You type them out carefully: "How about Tuesday at 2, Wednesday at 10, or Thursday at 3?" You press send. You feel productive. [pause]

The reply arrives. "Sorry, none of those work for me." [pause]

Breathe. [exhale]

This is not a rejection of you. This is a rejection of time itself. Tuesday did nothing wrong. Wednesday was innocent. Thursday was just trying to help.

You send three more times. They send three more times. None of them overlap. You begin to wonder if you and this person exist in the same dimension. You do. Your calendars do not. [long pause]

And here is the truth no one tells you: the meeting you are trying to schedule will last four minutes. Four minutes of content wrapped in thirty minutes of calendar block. You will both say "This was helpful" and neither of you will fully mean it. [pause]

The meeting was never about the meeting. It was about the intent. The gesture. The "let's connect" of it all.

Take one final breath. [exhale]

You are not your calendar. You are a person who exists between the blocks. And that space between is yours.

This has been Sigh. We'd love to schedule a follow-up, but none of our times work either.

### Share Card

> "You are not your calendar. You are a person who exists between the blocks. And that space between is yours."

## Session 11 — No Agenda
Welcome to Sigh.

You've just accepted a meeting. You don't know what it's about. There is no agenda. There is no description. There is only a title — something like "Sync" or "Touch Base" or simply your name and someone else's.

Take a breath. [exhale] You're going to be fine. Probably.

Let's talk about what you do know. You know the time. You know the room, or the Zoom link. You know who's invited. [pause] Look at the invite list. Are there people on it you don't recognize? That's okay. They don't know why they're here either.

No one knows why they're here. This is the nature of the modern meeting. It exists not because it needs to, but because someone felt the impulse to "align."

Take another breath. [exhale]

When the meeting begins, someone will share their screen. There will be a slide. The slide will say the same thing as the meeting title, which is to say: nothing. You will nod. [pause]

At some point, someone will ask, "Does anyone have any questions?" You do not. No one does. Questions require context, and context was not provided.

That's okay. You showed up. You were present. That is enough. [pause]

And when it ends five minutes early — and it will end five minutes early, because there was never enough content to fill it — allow yourself a moment of relief. You survived a meeting about nothing. Your record is perfect.

This has been Sigh. Agenda not included.

### Share Card

> "This is the nature of the modern meeting. It exists not because it needs to, but because someone felt the impulse to align."

## Session 12 — Incoming Call
Welcome to Sigh.

Your phone is ringing. Not a Slack notification. Not an email. Your phone. Someone is calling you. [pause]

Take a breath. [exhale] Look at the screen. It's your manager. Or your manager's manager. Or someone whose number you don't have saved but whose area code fills you with dread.

This is not normal. People do not call anymore. A phone call in 2026 is not a communication method. It is an escalation. [long pause]

Notice what's happening in your body right now. Your stomach has dropped. Your thumbs have frozen. You are calculating every mistake you've made in the past two weeks and ranking them by severity. This is natural.

Take another breath. [exhale]

You have a choice. You can answer, or you can let it ring. Both are valid. Answering is brave. Letting it go to voicemail is strategic. You'll tell yourself you were in a meeting. You were not in a meeting. That's okay. [pause]

If you answer, the first three seconds will be the longest of your career. "Hey, do you have a sec?" [long pause] You have never had less of a sec. But you will say yes. You always say yes.

And here is what no one tells you: it's almost never the thing you think it is. It's a question about a deadline. It's a heads-up about a reorg. It's your boss asking if you have the link to a Google Doc they could have searched for themselves.

It is almost never the thing. [pause]

Take one more breath. [exhale]

The call will end. Your heart rate will return to normal. And the next time your phone rings, you will spiral again, because that is what we do. And that's okay.

This has been Sigh. We would have texted, but this felt important.

### Share Card

> "This is not normal. People do not call anymore. A phone call in 2026 is not a communication method. It is an escalation."
